Why this page leads with AI

The Assistant Is
Doing the Shopping.

Customers are starting to ask an assistant instead of scrolling. Increasingly the assistant does not just answer — it goes and does the calling.

01
The customer stops calling around.

Google’s “have AI check pricing” — also called Ask for Me — is live for signed-in US users on mobile and desktop. Instead of ringing five businesses, the customer fills in a short form and lets Google’s AI make the calls.

02
Google’s AI phones local businesses.

It calls to gather pricing and availability, then emails or texts the customer a summary — typically within about 30 minutes. Google has confirmed the AI chooses which businesses to contact based on traditional local search rankings.

03
Rank decides if you are dialled. Answering decides if you are compared.

If nobody picks up, the AI falls back to whatever it can pull from your Business Profile or website. So the work is two-sided: rank in the places the AI reads, and be answerable when it arrives.

Categories seen so far include auto repair, hair and nail salons, pet groomers, wellness centres and driving schools, with a 2026 expansion into home services. Source: Google Search Help, “How to check pricing for local businesses with AI in Google Search.”

01
Assistant visibility monitoring

A fixed set of real customer prompts, run monthly across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity, logging whether you are mentioned and how you are described — tracked over time and reported monthly. Published ranges and tiers, so an AI caller — or an AI reading your site — gets a usable answer instead of “it depends”.

Coaching toward reviews that name the city, the service and the outcome. Specific reviews give an assistant context that generic praise does not.
